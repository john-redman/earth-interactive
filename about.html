About EarthInteractive

EarthInteractive is a free 3D globe that shows every country at its true size. Spin it, tap a country for facts, put two countries side by side and play a quick geography game.

Open the globe

Why a globe?

Most world maps use the Mercator projection. It keeps shapes and directions right, which is perfect for navigation, but it stretches everything far from the equator. Greenland looks as big as Africa. In reality Africa is about fourteen times larger. A globe is the only map with no such distortion, so EarthInteractive puts one in your browser and lets you lift countries off it to compare them directly. Read why flat maps lie.

What you can do

  • Explore. Tap or click any country for its capital, population, area, density, languages, currency and neighbours.
  • Compare true sizes. Pick two countries and they pop out as puzzle pieces you can drag anywhere on the globe. Move the United Kingdom over the United States, or Greenland over Africa.
  • Switch border views. Borders are political. Choose between the UN view, who actually controls each area, and a recognition-neutral view.
  • Colour the world by data. Population, density, wealth per person and area.
  • Play. The Daily Challenge gives everyone the same five countries each day. Find it is ten rounds that get harder.

New here? See how to play, browse all countries or start with a size comparison. There are also full rankings of the largest, most populous and most crowded countries.

Who makes it

EarthInteractive is an independent project built and run by John Redman, a developer who loves maps. There are no investors and no accounts. The source code is public on GitHub.

Where the data comes from

  • Borders and coastlines: Natural Earth (public domain).
  • Country facts such as capitals, languages, currencies and official areas: mledoze/countries, available under the Open Database License.
  • Population and GDP: World Bank World Development Indicators (CC BY 4.0), the latest year available for each country.
  • Live world population: an estimate from the UN World Population Prospects 2024 (medium variant): 8.23 billion people in mid-2025, about 4.2 births and 2.0 deaths a second.
  • Flags: flag-icons (MIT licence).

Borders shown on the globe are not an endorsement of any claim. The three border views exist precisely because people disagree about where some lines are.

Free to use
